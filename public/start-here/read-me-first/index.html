    Read Me First

    Three reading paths through Escapement: the machinist, the historian, and the buyer — and what each path will teach you.

    This site is one large, cross-linked argument. You can read it front to back or follow a thread. Here are the three threads that work best.

    Path 1 — The machinist (8 parts, ~90 minutes)

    You want to understand the object. Read the Anatomy series in order:

    1. The Problem of Timekeeping — why any of this is hard.
    2. The Gear Train — turning one rotation an hour into one a minute.
    3. The Mainspring and Barrel — the engine, and why it needs to lie about its own strength.
    4. The Escapement — the referee.
    5. The Balance Assembly — the oscillator that actually keeps time.
    6. The Keyless Works — winding and setting, one crown, two jobs.
    7. The Motion Works and Dial — how the hands are geared to disagree by exactly 12:1.
    8. Putting It Together — a full teardown in service order.

    Path 2 — The historian (one archive, ~60 minutes)

    You want the story of how we learned to split time into equal pieces. Start at the Timeline and follow it forward: Huygens and the pendulum (1656), the balance-spring race, Harrison’s longitude war, the American system, and the quartz crisis that nearly ended all of it.

    Path 3 — The buyer (practical, ~30 minutes)

    You own a watch or intend to. Read Amplitude (the health metric), Positions and Rate (why “it gains five seconds a day” is a normal sentence), Magnetism, Water Resistance, and Servicing. Then skim the Glossary before reading any watch forum ever again.

    How to use the links

    Every technical term that has its own page is linked the first time it appears. Following links is not a detour — it is how the site is meant to be read. The build also emits a Knowledge Graph page so you can see which pages lean on which.
